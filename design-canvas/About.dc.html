INSIDE, TAKING NOTES.

Deconstructing Academia is a public argument about knowledge: who gets to create it, whose language is treated as intelligent, whose experience counts as evidence, whose work gets cited, and who gets shut out before the conversation begins.

The point is not to make academia friendlier. The point is to make knowledge harder to hoard.

The Method

Every piece here runs the same basic questions, sometimes called The Second Look:

  1. What am I being shown as normal, natural, neutral, or inevitable?
  2. Who created the category, definition, statistic, or official story?
  3. What history had to disappear for this explanation to make sense?
  4. Whose experience, knowledge, labor, or suffering is missing?
  5. Who gains power, legitimacy, money, safety, or comfort when this story is accepted?
  6. What does reality look like when the missing information is restored?

Who's Asking

I'm Mabbit Rountree. My favorite color is glitter. Don't bullshit me — it's a color.

I live in Eugene, Oregon, with my husband, Chace, and three German Shepherds: mom, dad, and daughter. A complete canine family unit, because apparently one German Shepherd was not enough. We needed a dynasty.

I did not arrive at academia through the tidy, institutionally approved route. Before becoming a full-time student, I worked in warehouses, retail, gig delivery, and e-commerce. Now I'm a senior at the University of Oregon studying anthropology, and a freelance researcher whose work has run through public records, regulations, correctional facilities, and statistics that looked impressive until somebody asked what the denominator was.

I'm not standing outside academia throwing rocks at it. I'm inside, taking notes. And I brought receipts.

Editorial Values

  • Plainspoken. Skeptical. Funny when the absurdity earns it. Rigorous. Politically awake.
  • A citation supports the point — it doesn't become the personality.
  • No manufactured "both sides" paragraph when the evidence supports a conclusion.
  • Necessary nuance stays. Cowardly hedging goes.
I came to higher education with a life already in progress.

Learn the rules. Question who wrote them.
